About
I trained to price risk before I learned to model it. That order matters — and it's probably why you should hire me.
Explainable AI Risk & InsurTech Human Behaviour Policy Analytics

Here's the short version of who I am: I'm an actuary who learned to code, not a coder who picked up some statistics. That order matters more than it sounds. An actuary is trained from day one to ask "what happens if I'm wrong, and who pays for it?" before anything else. Most machine learning engineers never have to ask that question — I was taught nothing else but that question, for three years, before I ever touched a model.

Today I do both jobs at once. I'm a Research Scholar at iLabAfrica, Strathmore University, where my Master's research turns Kenya's national housing survey into a tool that can flag which households are financially at risk before a crisis hits. Alongside that, I run Vondetta, a small company I founded to build exactly this kind of decision-support AI for insurers, hospitals, and government offices across East Africa.

The one rule I hold myself to: if a client can't understand why my system made a decision, I haven't finished the job. A model that scores 95% accuracy but can't explain itself is not something I'll hand over to someone whose license, pricing, or health decisions depend on it.

I've already shipped, not just studied

More than once, my actuarial training has stopped a model from shipping with a hidden flaw — a leaked variable, a suspiciously perfect accuracy score, a stat that looked great but meant nothing. I'm trained to distrust a number until I understand why it's true.
The Journey

Not a list of job titles — the actual problems I was handed, and how I solved them, roughly in the order they happened.

Jun 2022 – Jul 2024
Claims Analyst · Kenyatta National Hospital
The problem
A national hospital processes an enormous number of insurance claims. Some are honest. Some aren't. Finding the second kind by hand, after the fact, is slow and it's already too late.
What I did
Built the data pipelines that cleaned and organised claims data, then used pattern-detection techniques to flag claims that looked statistically unusual — before they were paid out, not after.
In plain terms
This is where I learned that a model is only useful if it catches the problem in time to matter. A fraud flag raised six months later is just a post-mortem.
Aug – Dec 2024
Junior Underwriter · Incourage Insurance
The problem
Someone has to decide whether to accept an insurance application, and at what price. Get it wrong too often in either direction — too cautious or too generous — and the business loses money.
What I did
Applied statistical risk assessment to real applications, learning first-hand the judgment calls an underwriter has to defend — the exact judgment I now try to teach my AI systems to replicate.
In plain terms
This is where "explainable AI" stopped being a buzzword for me. I'd sat in the underwriter's chair. I knew exactly what a good explanation needed to sound like.
Dec 2024 – May 2025
Customer Success Assistant, AI & Data · Incourage Insurance
The problem
The company's AI chatbot was slow to answer and inconsistent, which meant customers gave up and called a human instead — expensive, and avoidable.
What I did
Analysed real customer conversations to find where the bot was failing, then redesigned its response logic.
In plain terms
Response time dropped by roughly a third. That's the difference between a customer who stays on the chat and one who hangs up and calls instead.
May 2025 – Present
Research Scholar · iLabAfrica, Strathmore University
